Why going cold turkey doesn't work

It's not a willpower problem. Videos and games are built so there's always “one more”: one video ends and the next starts on its own, every level hands out a reward.

Cutting everything at once lasts about two days. What works is cutting back gradually and always having something to offer instead of the screen.

What almost nobody tells you

When you set a new limit and stick to it, pushback often goes up for a few days before it goes down. Lots of families give up right there, in week 2. The book warns you ahead of time and gives you one sentence to keep repeating.

A free sample: what to say during a meltdown

You turn off the TV and it starts. Here's what chapter 3 says, word for word:

  1. Name what they're feeling: “You're really mad because you wanted to keep watching.”
  2. Hold the limit without yelling: “It's still off. There's more tomorrow.”
  3. Offer the next step: “Want to help me set the table, or should we go find the ball?”
  4. Wait. Sometimes it takes a while. If you give the screen back to calm them down, they learn that meltdowns work.
Ready to say

“I get it, it's hard to stop something you like. It's done for today. Now you choose: should we draw or go outside?”

Is this for you?

It's for you if

  • You have kids ages 2 to 12 and want less screen time without a fight every day.
  • You tried cutting screens cold turkey and two days later everything was back to normal.
  • You want concrete ideas, not theory: what to say, what to do and what to set up.
  • You can give it a few minutes a day for a month.

It's not for you if

  • You want them off screens in 7 days. This is gradual.
  • Your child is under 2: the book starts at age 2.
  • Something about their health or behavior worries you: that needs a professional.
  • You want rules for the kids only: here the grown-ups put the phone down a bit too.
